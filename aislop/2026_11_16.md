## 1\. Your Spanish sentence

**Quiero una botella de agua grande.**

Your translation:

> I want to have large bottle with water.

A more natural English translation is:

> I want a large bottle of water.

Word by word:

| Spanish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Quiero | I want |
| una | a (feminine) |
| botella | bottle |
| de | of |
| agua | water |
| grande | big / large |

Notice something interesting?

Spanish doesn't need a separate word for "I" here.

## 2\. Why is "Quiero" equivalent to "I want"?

This is called **subject omission** or _pro-drop_.

Spanish verbs change their endings depending on who performs the action.

For example:

| Spanish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Yo quiero | I want |
| Tú quieres | You want |
| Él quiere | He wants |
| Ella quiere | She wants |
| Nosotros queremos | We want |
| Ellos quieren | They want |

The verb ending already tells you who is performing the action.

Therefore:

- Yo quiero = I want.
- Quiero = I want.

Both are grammatically correct.

You don't need to say _yo_ every time.

Interestingly, English doesn't work this way. We generally need an explicit subject.

## 3\. Why "botella de agua" instead of "agua botella"?

Spanish uses:

**Noun + de + Noun**

This construction often expresses what something contains, what it is made of, or its relationship to something else.

Examples:

| Spanish | English |
| --- | --- |
| Botella de agua | Bottle of water |
| Taza de café | Cup of coffee |
| Libro de español | Spanish book |
| Vaso de leche | Glass of milk |
| Casa de madera | Wooden house |

Think of _de_ as a relationship connector.

It doesn't always translate to "of", but that's a useful starting point.

## 4\. Why "grande" comes after "botella"?

This is another important difference.

In English, adjectives usually come before nouns:

- Large bottle.
- Beautiful flower.
- Cheap hotel.

In Spanish, adjectives often come after nouns:

- Botella grande.
- Flor bonita.
- Hotel barato.

So:

- Grande botella ❌ (not the normal neutral word order)
- Botella grande ✅

However, Spanish adjectives can sometimes appear before nouns, depending on emphasis and meaning.

## 5\. Something interesting: "agua" is feminine, but uses "el"

Here's a little Spanish curiosity.

We normally say:

- El agua (the water)
- Una botella (a bottle)
- Una botella de agua (a bottle of water)

Why _el agua_ instead of _la agua_?

Because feminine singular nouns beginning with a stressed _a_ sound generally use _el_ as their singular definite article.

But the noun remains feminine\!

That's why we say:

**El agua fría.**

Not _el agua frío_.

The adjective remains feminine.
